APPENDIX

Will people keep paying? What about refunds?

  • They don’t have to: the first sale pays back acquisition (₩54,000 contribution vs. ₩25,000 target CAC).
  • Pads are used up, so the ₩4,900 monthly plan (pads + coaching) has a real reason to stay.
  • Refunds: 7-day test, refund minus shipping. Still profitable while refunds stay under about 30%. The free app filters who buys, and returned devices are reused.
  • US on Amazon: $69 − 15% fee − ~$18 device − ~$6 fulfillment ≈ $35 left before ads (~51%).
  • Market: $1.1–1.8B global, $170M US positional therapy. Goal: 10,000 users in 3 years.

Sources: Coherent MI, DelveInsight, SNS Insider (2025); Ravesloot (2017); Amazon referral fees (Beauty, Health & Personal Care); Feedvisor FBA example. Device cost at ₩1,400/$. Pad cost to be confirmed.

APPENDIX

Why on the back of the shoulder?

  • You only feel it when you lie on your back. On your side, it stays out of the way.
  • One coin-sized device: motion sensor, buzz motor, Bluetooth, battery. The phone does the listening.
  • It snaps onto a medical gel pad. Only the pad touches the skin and gets replaced.
  • Returned devices are cleaned, tested and reused, so refunds cost less.
  • You can put it on by yourself, and it stays put. A chest band slides around.

APPENDIX

Why not just a smartwatch app?

  • Your wrist doesn’t know which way your torso faces; your arm moves on its own.
  • Clinically tested position trainers are worn on the chest or the back of the neck.
  • Watches screen over weeks (Apple: 10 nights in a 30-day period). They don’t intervene tonight.
  • We see watches as our funnel, not our competitor.

Sources: van Maanen et al. (2013); Apple sleep apnea notification, FDA-cleared 2024

APPENDIX

Won’t the vibration wake people up?

  • One month of vibration position therapy: sleep efficiency did not change significantly, and daytime sleepiness dropped.
  • Compared with the tennis-ball technique: fewer awakenings and less time awake.
  • We buzz less often: only when you snore on your back, weakest first.

Sources: van Maanen et al., Sleep and Breathing (2013); Eijsvogel et al., J Clin Sleep Med (2015)

APPENDIX

Is this a medical device?

  • Phase 1: a wellness snoring aid with no medical claims, as Smart Nora is sold (“not indicated for diagnosis or treatment of sleep apnea”).
  • The FDA’s January 2026 wellness guidance treats non-invasive sleep trackers as low-risk. We still have our claim wording reviewed.
  • Phase 2: FDA 510(k) with Night Shift (K140190) as the comparison device. It was cleared on a 27-patient study. Then Medicare billing through DME partners.

Sources: SleepScore Labs (Smart Nora indication); FDA General Wellness guidance (Jan 2026); FDA K140190 summary; Sleep Review (CMS coverage)

Can’t someone just copy this?
